Two crashes, both found in Sentry rather than by anyone using the app, and both on screens that
are hard to avoid.

## The profile screen could show an error instead of a health card

`Bad state: No element`, thrown out of a widget's `build` during layout — unhandled, fatal, and on
the screen the app opens to. Two people hit it fourteen times in a day.

The health grid asks for the metrics that have at least a day of data, then builds a card for each.
Both halves were right; the timing between them was not. A sliver builds its children lazily, so the
list of metrics was settled at one moment and each card looked its own data up at a later one. The
background backfill empties that store and refills it, and a card that got built in the gap found
nothing where its own metric's history had been, then asked for the last entry of it.

The card now receives the data it was chosen for, rather than fetching it again when it comes to be
built. There is no longer a gap for the backfill to land in — the count and the data are the same
snapshot.

## Declining notifications no longer counts as a crash

Turning Heart's notifications off, then finishing a workout, was recorded as a fatal error. iOS
refuses to store a notification for an app that has not been granted permission, and nothing was
listening for that refusal, so it surfaced as a crash on the screen that ends a session — for doing
exactly what the permission prompt offers.

Nothing about it was ever visible: the workout saved, the app carried on, and only the crash report
knew. It is now treated as the answer it is. Every *other* scheduling failure still gets reported,
which is the point of fixing it this way rather than silencing the call — a notification that cannot
be drawn is our bug, and that one has shipped before.

## Web

Google sign-in on the web renders Google's own button, which it was written to do and had never been
connected to do. The web build also compiles again, after a dependency update left two halves of
Firebase's web authentication out of step.

Neither reaches the App Store or Play; recorded because it is in this range.

## Releasing

TestFlight builds no longer lose a coin flip on the way out. Fastlane's patience with Xcode is tuned
for projects that resolve their dependencies differently to this one, and on a slow machine it gave
up before the build began — which is what happened to the first attempt at the previous release.
