# Keys: what end-to-end encryption promises, and to whom

Decided with the owner on 2026-11-25, after three reviews of #756/#750 kept finding holes that the
previous fixes had opened. Code about keys is built against this page; a review finding that
contradicts it is a question for this page first. It narrows "Messages and safety" in
`2026-10-31.coaching.md` where the two differ.

## Who we protect against

- **The server, and anyone who reads its database.** It carries keys and messages and can read
  neither. It's trusted to deliver, never to say whose a key is: a key is taken only on proof a device
  checks itself (a fingerprint, a compared code, a signature by a device it already trusts).
- **Someone holding only the password.** They can sign in and add a device. That device reads nothing
  already said. What it can do is below, with what each side sees.
- **A removed device,** stolen or sold. Once removed, it reads nothing written after.

Not covered: a phone that is unlocked in someone else's hands, and a person who shows their screen.

## Trust is people, not only devices

Coach and student meet. They will send each other form videos and voice messages, and they know each
other's faces and voices. Devices are one way to know who's on the other end, not the only one. So:

- **New devices aren't blocked, they're announced.** When the other person adds a device, it gets new
  messages at once, and the thread says so: "Muffin added a device", and whether one of their own
  devices approved it. Nothing is held back waiting for a check.
- **Checking in person is offered, not required.** Either side can compare a code with the other when
  they meet, and the thread then shows that device as checked. A device that wasn't approved by its own
  owner and wasn't checked in person is marked as such wherever its messages show.
- **The invitation's fingerprint stays.** A student's app still checks the coach's devices against the
  link they were invited by, and the devices that one approved.

What a password holder gets, then: new messages from the other person, from the moment their device is
added, with the thread saying a new device joined that the owner didn't approve. Never anything older.

## Losing a phone doesn't lose what was said

"I shouldn't lose my stuff just because my only phone was stolen."

- **A recovery key.** An account can keep an encrypted backup of its message and note keys on the
  server, locked by a recovery key only the person holds. The server can't open it. A new device that
  has the recovery key reads everything the backup covers, with no other device present.
- It's offered when coaching messages are first used, once, and can be set up later from Your devices.
  Without it, the current rule stands: a new device with no approved device left starts empty.
- This reverses "no recovery key in the first version" in the coaching design: losing your only phone
  turned out to be the case that matters most.

## Removing a device

- **Any device of the account may remove any other,** after a warning that says what it does. No one
  is stuck at the ten-device limit because the devices that approved things are gone.
- Removing announces itself: the account's other devices are told which device removed which, and say
  so in Your devices. A removal nobody recognises is how a person learns their password is known.
- **A removed device stops getting keys, and the keys it held are replaced** before anything else is
  sealed. When it learns it was removed, it forgets its keys; it comes back only when asked, as a new
  device.
- A device that a removed one had approved needs approving again.

## Passing keys on

- Only among the account's own approved devices, and only with proof of which device made the key.
  The other person never passes on keys that aren't theirs.
- A recovery key covers what passing on can't: keys whose only holder is gone.

## What the server needs

- The device listing says when a device was removed and by which device (heart-api#483).
- A device approved by a removed one can be approved again (heart-api#484).
- An account can store and replace one encrypted key backup, and read it back (requested with this page).
