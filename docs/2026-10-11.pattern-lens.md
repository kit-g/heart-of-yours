# The pattern lens: movement data under charts, goals and programs

It does not matter that you bench six sets a week. It matters whether you press six times a
week, and whether you pull as often. Every library exercise already knows what it is: a
`Movement` with one or more of 38 pattern groups (`horizontal_press`, `hip_hinge`, …) and five
load attributes (axial load, stability, unilateral, impact, skill), plus `MuscleTagging` with
primary and secondary muscles. The library filters on it, the About tab shows it, and
`alternativesTo` in `heart_state` ranks substitutes by it. Nothing that counts uses it.

Every `GoalMetric` but `workouts` is keyed to one exercise. The charts mirror the goals. Volume
per muscle (#136) is the one cross-exercise view, and it is a report, not a thing to aim at or
to watch over time. This note is the layer that fixes that, and Programs
(`docs/2026-10-11.programs.md`) is specified against it.

## Rules

1. **Two keys over the same sets.** A pattern and a muscle are two ways to group the sets of a
   week. Aggregation is one function with a key, not two features. Everything below takes
   either key.
2. **Frequency and sets, per week.** The two numbers people program by: how many sessions
   touched the key, and how many sets landed on it. Neither exists today. Volume stays per
   muscle because a kilogram on a hinge and on a curl are not the same kilogram.
3. **Balance is the first visible payoff.** Push against pull, hinge against squat, left
   against right through `unilateral`. No exercise chart can say any of it. One view, two bars
   per pair.
4. **A goal takes a subject.** Exercise, pattern or muscle. "Press twice a week" is the
   `workouts` goal with a pattern key; "12 sets of pull a week" is a sets goal with the same
   key. The server stores the definition and the app computes progress, as today.
5. **A pattern has a best.** The best estimated one-rep max among the pattern's exercises.
   Swapping barbell bench for dumbbells loses nothing, and two people on different programs
   stay comparable.
6. **Keys are content.** The 38 pattern keys are snake_case identifiers owned by the exercise
   library and can grow without a release. Nothing in the app hard-codes the list or needs a
   hand-written label per key. The load attributes are small closed enums and every real use
   is a ceiling (`atMost`).
7. **Unannotated exercises fall to the muscle key.** User-created exercises carry no
   `Movement`, so the pattern lens does not see them. The muscle key still does. When an
   exercise is created, ask for its pattern once, from the library's keys; never block on it.
8. **Whole sets only.** Secondary muscles do not count as half a set. A set counts for its
   primary key, and a secondary view, if one is ever wanted, is a separate toggle, never a
   decimal in a chart.

## What builds on it

- Programs: the week's coverage ("3 presses, 0 pulls"), the one-line rationale per session,
  replace-within-pattern, program-scoped constraints ("this program avoids high axial load"),
  and Together's comparison through pattern bests.
- Goals: a subject on the goal, and the balance goals that fall out of it.
- Charts: a series per key, sets and sessions a week, current period over previous (#137).
- The open item from the movement work: pattern coverage on a workout or template.

## Not decided

- Whether a pattern best counts assisted and weighted bodyweight variants in the same
  pattern. It probably does not.
- Whether coverage reads sessions or sets by default. Sessions is the simpler sentence.
