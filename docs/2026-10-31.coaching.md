# Coaching: the design

A draft. A coach sends programs, sees whether they were done, and talks to the person doing them.
The student keeps the app they know and stays in charge of their own data. The boards are on the
Heart Coaching design canvas (a private claude.ai design artifact); this document holds the
decisions. The ticket is #168. The backend side is `docs/2026-11-01.coaching.md` in heart-api, which
is accepted, and this document follows it. Coaching goes out as a free beta to people who test it
with us, then as a release: moderation is in, payment is out. One thing here is ahead of it: the web as
a full invitation surface. Competitor research stays in private
notes.

## Who it is for

A coach with a few clients and a coach with hundreds, and the people they train, whether that is
someone preparing for a meet or someone getting back after an accident. Heart never prescribes:
the coach does, and Heart keeps the plan and the counts.

## Two spaces, one app

Coaching is a second space, not more tabs. Your own space is Profile, Workout, History, Exercises,
exactly as it was, with one Coaching button on Profile. The Coaching space replaces the tabs with
Clients, Messages, Programs and Me, so a coach's work and their own training never share a screen.

- The app stays in the space you left it in, across launches.
- A push about a client opens Coaching. Nothing about a client appears in your own space.
- The programs you send live in Coaching, not among your own Templates.
- Your own workout can keep running in Coaching: one line says so, and takes you back to it.
- On a tablet the switch sits at the bottom of the rail.

A student sees no second space and no new tab. A coach who also has a coach has both.

## Opt-in

Two features, under `docs/opt-in.md`, because they are two different people's choices.

- **Coach people** is switched on in Settings › Features, with no ask. Off, the Coaching button is
  gone and the app looks as if it had never been built. In the beta it only appears for an account
  holding the `coach` entitlement, which we grant by hand, and the profile says whether it holds it.
- **Your coach** asks when an invitation arrives, and shows in Features only after that. Anyone with
  a link can accept. Off pauses the connection: the coach stops seeing new workouts and is told it is
  paused. A student who turned coaching off while data kept flowing would be misled. Only the student
  can resume it.

Off keeps the data, as ever. Coaching needs an account, so an anonymous user gets that step first.

## Joining

A coach makes a link for one named person. It works once, for 7 days, and resending renews both the
link and the 7 days. There is no search over people: a coach account is not a directory of users. The
coach can pick a program to start with, so nothing waits on the person accepting. A student already
at their program limit still connects: the coach sees that the program didn't arrive, and can send it
later. Coach connections only ever come from an invitation.

An invitation names a domain, and a pair holds one relationship per domain. So two people can be peers
in one and coach and student in another, and each can coach the other in a different domain. Someone
coached in two domains appears twice on the roster. If you end a relationship, only a new invitation
reopens it, and the coach then reads from the new date, with nothing shared until the student says so.

The link opens the app if it's installed, and otherwise a web page. The web is a full invitation
surface, not a stop on the way to a store: inviting and accepting work there as they do on a phone,
including signing in. A link stays valid until someone accepts it, so someone who installs Heart first
can tap it again.

Someone who accepts on the web may have no device holding encryption keys yet. Until they do, the coach
can see the connection and send a program, but the thread says it's waiting for them to open Heart on a
device. The link's key fingerprint has to reach that device too: opening Heart through the same link
carries it. Opened any other way, the app has nothing to check the coach's key against, and it says so
in the thread until the two compare a code.

The ask says, on either surface and before the yes, what the coach will see, what they can do, and what they won't see.
Sharing starts at acceptance. The student can also share two more things, off unless they say so and
changeable at any time, only by them: their earlier workouts, and their goals. A link never makes a
connection on its own. The ask can also block the inviter, even though no connection exists yet, and a
blocked coach's next link looks like any link that's no longer good.

Coaches will want a public link to post on their social feeds. That comes after the first version, once
abuse handling exists, and the API leaves room for it. Anyone can open such a link, so it only lets
someone ask: the coach approves each person, and the student still sees the same ask before any sharing
starts.

## What a coach sees

Workouts logged from acceptance on, with their notes and without their photos; the program and how it's
going; the name and photo the student uses in Heart. With the student's say, earlier workouts, and goals
over workout metrics only.

A student can hide any one workout, and hiding works on peers too. Workouts started during a pause
stay out as well. A coach sees that a session was skipped, repeated or paused, never the reason given:
`sick` and `sore` are health-shaped, so this is a filter on what the coach reads. The server stores
those reasons today, and only the go-easy list and health readings are refused outright.

**What a coach sees that's worked out is worked out only from what they can read.** That covers
position, bases, the roster and the feed, since a hidden workout must not show up as the week moving on.
So a hidden program workout leaves its session undone in the coach's view, and the coach's week can trail
the student's real one. The student's own position doesn't change.

Comments and references follow the same rule: anything the coach can't read is simply not there.

A coach's view beats a peer's. While someone holds a live or paused coach relationship with a person,
also being their peer adds nothing, and a pause in one domain stops coach reads in all of them. A
peer reads what a peer reads today, photos included.

Never: anything from Apple Health or Health Connect, go-easy areas, skip reasons, progress photos,
an email address. Under `docs/2026-09-05.health-data.md` nothing health-derived reaches a coach, and the
pitch says so plainly. `site/privacy.html` has to change before any tester reaches this: it says we
don't share.

## The plan and the log stay separate

This is the design's centre. A program's sessions hold the targets; workouts hold what happened.

- **Finishing a workout never changes a plan.** No setting, no prompt.
- **Both people can change a session,** and every change names who made it and what it was. The
  student's veto from `docs/2026-10-11.programs.md` holds: it is their body. The coach can keep a
  student's change or put it back, and putting back a deleted session also relinks its workouts and
  skips, so the position returns.
- **Conflicts are per session, and only with the other person.** A coach's edit to Monday doesn't stop
  a student's edit to Thursday, and your own queued edits never conflict with each other. When the other
  person changed the same session first, the app gets `409 program_changed` and has to show both. An
  old build can't handle that, so its write lands and is marked stale, and putting it back is one action.
- **A coach's edit arrives without asking,** since that is what they were hired for, but never into
  a running workout. The band says what changed.
- **A new program asks when to start,** because it replaces what the student is on.
- **Each student has their own copy.** It arrives without bases, and the student's app proposes them
  from their history, as when starting from the library. Your library program isn't touched by a change
  for one client, and a change to it reaches nobody until you send it. The program page shows who is on
  an older copy, and updating them is one action. An update keeps the ids of what the student has
  trained, so their week and bases hold, and it overwrites their own edits to that copy as a recorded
  change that can be put back. The app has to say so before the coach does it.
- **Ending a relationship ends the coach's edits** to the program sent through it, and leaves any other
  domain alone.

## Reviewing a workout

The question is "did they do what I asked?", and the card answers it without opening anything else:
target beside done, per exercise, with what was lighter, skipped or added in words, and the
student's note above it.

What was asked can't be read from the stored session, because lighter cycles and wave rules change it,
and doing the planned 2 of 4 sets in a lighter week would read as 2 not done. So the app records the
planned session on the workout when it's saved, and ties each set to the planned set it came from. A
workout with no plan, from an old build or not from a program, has no comparison and raises no reason.

The roster sorts by who needs a word, with the reason in words: a session lighter than planned, a
note, a gap, a program ending next week. Reasons need an end, so the app tells the server when a coach
has reviewed a client, and only what came after counts. Gaps and endings clear when they stop being
true. The roster may lag a little. A long one becomes a table on a large screen, with filters for those
reasons. Seven days as dots tell the rest, worked out by the app from training times, since the server
knows no time zones. "Needs a word" is for the coach, and a student never sees it. No streaks, and no
nudges to the student.

## Messages and safety

One thread per connection, text only in the first version, in one inbox tab. Images and video come
later, and nothing here is in their way. A message can be about any detail of a workout, down to one
set, or a skip, a program or a session. That reference sits beside the sealed text, so a new tablet can
find what was said about that set, and the server learns which set is being talked about, which it
holds anyway, and never what's said. A reference opens nothing: a message about a workout later hidden
stays, and the workout stays hidden. There's one inbox, and no likes in the first version. Reactions
can follow in the same shape (heart-api#369).

**Messages are end-to-end encrypted from the first one,** and no version in the clear is built or
released. People will write about their knee in a thread, which is the same health data coach notes
hold, so a thread readable on our server would break the rule that keeps it off. Only the two people can
read a thread.

- **Pushes carry no content:** "Alex messaged you". So they need no decryption on the device.
- **Search runs on the device.** The assistant, `/me` and MCP can't read messages.
- **A second device** is let in by one you already have, comparing a code. With no other device to
  ask, a new one starts empty: earlier messages and notes can't be opened. There is no recovery key
  in the first version, because that is one more thing to lose. A direct local transfer between
  devices, like AirDrop, can be considered later.
- **The library is `vodozemac`** (Rust, Apache-2.0), through a thin binding of our own, since its
  Dart bindings are AGPL-3.0. Never our own protocol. `openmls` wasn't needed.
- **The server keeps the thread, unreadable.** Each message is stored once, and every device that
  holds the thread's keys can read it, in any order and more than once. That's how a coach's new
  tablet shows the whole thread once it's let in, and it leaves room for a recovery key later. The
  keys reach each device sealed for that device alone. They change when a device is removed, when a
  connection ends and from time to time, and a device gets only the ones it's entitled to. The
  cost is that a stolen key opens everything it covers, including old messages the server still
  holds, where a key used once and deleted would not. This is the same arrangement Matrix uses.
- **Order is the server's.** The app reads by the server's cursor and shows the device's time, so a
  message written offline can't fall behind someone who has already read past that point. Unread is
  what's after your read position from the other person.
- **A device sends key items one at a time,** waiting for each answer, because that's what keeps them
  in order. A full queue answers `400 queue_full`.
- **Keys stay out of phone backups.** A phone restored from an old backup reuses encryption keys it
  has already used, and the other side can't read it. A restored phone counts as a new device.
- **Who you're talking to.** The server hands out keys, so it could hand out the wrong ones. An
  invitation link carries the coach's key fingerprint in the part of the address that never reaches a
  server, and the student's app checks it. A new device is vouched for by an existing one, and the
  thread says when the other person has added one, and whether it was vouched for. The spike didn't
  build this.
- **Stores.** Crypto not provided by the OS makes `ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption` in `Info.plist`
  untrue. Apple asks for a French encryption declaration for an industry-standard algorithm not
  provided by the OS, when distributing in France. Check this before any store build carries it,
  including what TestFlight requires.
- **Review.** The report check and how the pieces fit together are our own work, on a published idea,
  and need a qualified review before release.

**Either person can delete the conversation, for both sides.** One call, whatever the connection's
status, and no agreement from the other side. It's the whole conversation, not one message. The server
removes the messages at once, and the thread can take new ones if the connection is live. Every device
learns from the thread and drops what it holds. The other person gets a short window, so that deleting
can't destroy the evidence before a report: the app shows who deleted it and keeps its copy for 7 days,
for reporting only, then drops it. A filed report isn't touched.

**Later, sealed things beyond messages.** A student's device could seal a skip's reason or a go-easy area
for their coach, and the server would store a string it can't read. That needs no new table or route, only
a new allowed kind, and the first version allows `message` alone. Sealing doesn't lift the health rule:
`CLAUDE.md`, the health doc and `site/privacy.html` have to change first, and that's an explicit policy
decision.

**Blocking and reporting.** Report and block are in the thread's menu, on the ask, and on the coach page.
A block belongs to the pair, across every domain and every path. It stops sharing at once and stops
messages and new invitations. The blocker can still read the thread, and the blocked person can't.

A report carries the reporter's reason and the words their device sends, with what's needed to check them.
The server checks them, never stores them in clear, and keeps them sealed to a key whose private half
isn't on the server. We open them with a script, since a review app is out of the first version. A closed
report is deleted after 90 days and an unclosed one after a year, and neither person deleting their
account removes it. Apple's 1.2 and Google's equivalent ask for a way to report, a timely response, a way
to block and a contact route, which is `site/support.html`.

A coach's words can't act on a student's assistant: `list_workouts`, `get_workout`, `list_programs`,
`get_program` and `get_program_history` return them marked as quoted. That ships with the first thing a
coach can send.

## Notes on a client

Coaches keep injury notes. A note about someone's knee, readable on our server, is health data by
another route, so **a coach's notes are end-to-end encrypted, with the coach as the only reader**.
They sync across the coach's own devices, and the server holds only ciphertext. The client never learns
they exist. They stay when a client leaves or deletes their account, until the coach deletes them or the
coach's account goes. `docs/2026-09-05.health-data.md` and `site/privacy.html` need a line: this is the
first health-shaped data with a remote copy, even an unreadable one. What a program must act on goes on
the program, as a constraint such as "avoid high axial load", which says nothing about why.

## Logging in person

The sheet, marked "Logging for Muffin". The result is the student's workout, marked "Logged by
Alex", which the student can see and delete. It is never public. It carries a plan like any other
workout, a coach's custom exercise comes along with it, and the coach's device works out the base move
with the same code the student's would, so later weights stay right.

## A program ending

The coach hears a week ahead and can send what's next, add weeks or let it end. A student who
finishes keeps the program, can start it again heavier, and is told their coach knows. Nothing reads
as a failure.

## Pushes

They say who, never what: no message text, no weights, no health values, including for comments. A
coach's workout news comes once a day, and a message rings at once. Coaching ships the first push client
this app has had, and nothing has ever reached a phone until then.

The settings screen is drawn from the server's answer, so a build that has never heard of a type of push
can still show and change it, and a new type costs no app release. Delivery can be instant, daily or off,
per type and per person, and the most specific answer wins. The app sends its UTC offset with its device
token, and the most recently seen device decides when a person's evening is. Silent pushes tell the app
that a program changed or that the other person added a device.

## Leaving

One step, open to either side, and immediate. The student keeps the program as their own, with no further
coach edits, plus their history and the thread. The coach keeps that the person was a client, the dates,
the thread and their notes. They lose everything else, and the app drops what it cached of that person's
workouts and program.

Either person deleting the conversation takes the thread from both, as above. Deleting an account leaves
the conversation with the other person, so anyone who wants their words gone deletes the conversation
first, and the account deletion screen and `site/privacy.html` have to say so.

A coach counts as someone a student shows things to, not as a separate keeper of them.

## Large screens

Coaching is designed for them first, because a coach's work is comparing things. Panes follow the
measured width (`CLAUDE.md`, *Large screens*), not the device:

| Width | Layout | Where |
|---|---|---|
| under 600 | one pane | phone, Duo closed (466) |
| 600 to 900 | list and client | Duo open (669), tablet upright |
| over 900 | list, client, and messages, program or notes | tablet on its side |

- **The size is read live.** A Duo changes size in the hand, and today's startup split reads the
  display once (#327). Coaching can't ship on that.
- **A fold keeps your place:** the same client, scroll position and unsent message.
- **The same room helps at the gym:** on a Duo or tablet the sheet sits beside last time and your
  notes. A student gets the sheet beside the coach's messages.
- **The editor is three panes:** sessions, the session, and an open exercise picker.

This may spare Heart a web dashboard while the web app is in doubt. That is the idea most worth
testing with a working coach.

## What the server needs

This repo's request is `docs/2026-10-31.coaching-api.md`. heart-api answered it with
`docs/2026-11-01.coaching.md`, built it in heart-api#358 to #366 and #375 (the web), and handed it over in
#552 to #560, each with its contract. `docs/2026-11-07.coaching-api.md` in heart-api describes the API as
built.

That API isn't final. `/me` and MCP surfaces are still to come, and anything the app finds it needs
while building goes back to heart-api as a change request, as do new features. Build to what the
handover tickets describe, and keep the wire behind `heart_api` so a changed contract changes
one place.

## Decided against

Age checks. Heart doesn't ask for an age, estimate one or infer one, and coaching changes nothing here.

## Later

Teams, images and video, `/me` and MCP surfaces for coaching, a review app for
reports, limits for release. The beta's limits are guards that nobody is expected to reach.

## Payment, when it comes

Out of the beta, and kept here so it isn't lost: per session rather than brackets, with caps that
follow the competing brackets, so a coach pays for sessions trained and never more than elsewhere.
As many ways to pay as is reasonable, with the web cheaper than in-app.

## The encryption spike

Run on 2026-10-31 and 2026-11-01.

A throwaway Flutter app, with no plugin, called `vodozemac` through a small binding and a build hook
that runs cargo. A real iPhone and a real Pixel 7 held a thread through a stand-in server, with a
simulated iPad as the coach's second device. The server's log held only ciphertext for keys and
messages. The code is in `env/e2e-trial/` (gitignored).

- **Proven on the iPhone and Pixel:** a stored thread both ways, keys read back from the Keychain and
  Keystore across restarts, three messages read after one side was offline, the whole thread read
  newest first and twice over, a new thread key on every launch with the old ones still reading, and
  reports: a genuine one passed, and altered words and a blamed third person failed.
- **Proven with the iPad simulator:** a new device let in by the iPhone, with the same code on both
  screens, received every earlier thread key, read the whole thread, and sent. The iPhone's signature
  on its key checked out, and the same signature on another key failed. The comparison ran
  automatically, with no person pressing a button.
- **Proven on the Pixel with simulators on the other side:** an invite-link fingerprint that matched,
  and a swapped key from the server that was refused; a lost thread key asked for again and received
  from another device; a coach's note sealed on one device and read on the second; a stale session
  that couldn't open a message, then started over; and state put back to an older copy, which a
  counter in secure storage noticed before the next send, after which the thread carried on both
  ways.
- **Proven in Rust tests only:** a code comparison that a server in the middle can't fake, and that
  the library opens two messages at one position, so the caller has to refuse the second. The app
  did refuse one.
- **Speed:** encrypting and saving a message took 1 to 8 ms on the phones. On the Pixel, 1,000
  500-character messages sealed in 244 ms and opened newest first in 163 ms.
- **Limits:** per-device items must be read in order, since only 40 skipped ones can be recovered, and
  a pair more than about 2,000 items behind needs a new session. A device holds at most 50 single-use
  keys.
- **Cost:** about 0.8 MB on Android and 0.7 MB on iOS for each architecture (the iOS figure is
  estimated from an earlier build), and 7 seconds per target with
  downloads cached. Rust 1.89 or later is needed on every build and test, unless the package finds a
  prebuilt library, which it uses instead. That worked on macOS. CI and the agents' Linux container
  haven't tried it.
- **Web:** the library compiles for the browser at 100 KB. There's no Dart binding for that, and no
  answer yet on where a browser can keep keys safely.
- **Not done:** an actual backup and restore through iOS or Android, since state was put back by hand;
  a person comparing codes; screens for any of this. The iPhone didn't rerun the last round, because
  it needs a person to allow local network access each install, though it's the same library. The
  library has one audit, and fixed two CVEs in 2024.

### The package

Everything to do with encryption lives in one package, because another app may need it. It knows
nothing about Heart, coaching, any server or any store: its caller supplies where state is kept, how
things travel, and the app's own context strings, and it depends on no Heart package. The spike's
version is `sealbox`, and it was the build that ran on the phones. Its README lists what a caller
must do because the library won't. It should be able to offer both
ways of keeping messages: a stored thread, which is what coaching wants, and per-device copies whose
keys are deleted after one use, which protect old messages better. Which one fits is the app's call.
