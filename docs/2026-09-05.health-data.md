# Health data — policy and behaviour

What Heart does with the data it reads from Apple Health (HealthKit) and Google
Health Connect, stated once, for anyone touching the feature or the privacy
policy. The decision record and the war stories are in
`docs/2026-08-02.wearables.md`; this file is the contract that came out of it.

The public statement of the same policy is `site/privacy.html` in the heart-api
repo, section *Health data*. **The two must agree.** Any change to what is read,
written, stored or reported also changes that page (see *Change protocol*).

## The policy

1. **Health data never leaves the device.** Not raw samples, not aggregates, not
   rollups, not goals over health metrics, not error messages that quote a
   value, not screenshots that show one. There is no server table, no API
   endpoint, and no `synced` column for anything health-related.
2. **Read only what is shown.** Every metric read is one the app renders and one
   the user is asked to grant. The list is `Health.tracked` in
   `shared/heart_state/lib/src/health.dart`; adding to it is a product decision
   with a paperwork trail, not a refactor.
3. **Write exactly one thing back: the workout the user just finished.** Its
   start, end and activity type, plus a title on Android. No energy, no
   distance, no heart rate — Heart measured none of them and will not invent
   them.
4. **Ask at the point of use, never on launch.** The read permission is asked
   from the dashboard invitation or the settings row; the write permission at
   the first finish, and only for a user who has already engaged with health.
5. **Treat a permission as unknowable on iOS.** HealthKit does not disclose
   read authorisation. UI branches on `Health.hasData`, never on a permission
   check, and never renders a "connected" state.
6. **No health data for advertising, profiling, or anything but showing it back
   to the person it belongs to.** Apple's guideline 5.1.3 and Google's Health
   Connect policy both require this; the device-only rule satisfies it by
   construction.
7. **The OS store is the backup, and Heart's copy is disposable.** Heart keeps a
   local mirror so charts draw offline; losing it costs nothing because the
   next sync re-reads the store.

## What is read

Six metrics, read on every launch, on resume (throttled to once per fifteen
minutes) and after a permissions trip, back to September 2014 or as far as the
store goes.

| `HealthMetric`         | HealthKit type                | Health Connect permission             | Stored unit |
|------------------------|-------------------------------|---------------------------------------|-------------|
| `restingHeartRate`     | `RESTING_HEART_RATE`          | `READ_RESTING_HEART_RATE`             | bpm         |
| `heartRateVariability` | `HEART_RATE_VARIABILITY_SDNN` | `READ_HEART_RATE_VARIABILITY` (RMSSD) | ms          |
| `sleepAsleep`          | `SLEEP_ASLEEP`                | `READ_SLEEP`                          | minutes     |
| `steps`                | `STEPS`                       | `READ_STEPS`                          | count       |
| `activeEnergy`         | `ACTIVE_ENERGY_BURNED`        | `READ_ACTIVE_CALORIES_BURNED`         | kcal        |
| `bodyMass`             | `WEIGHT`                      | `READ_WEIGHT`                         | kg          |

`HealthMetric` also names heart rate, body fat and the sleep stages. They are
vocabulary, not permissions: nothing requests or reads them until they join
`Health.tracked`, and the Android manifest deliberately lists only the six above.

Each sample keeps its **source** (writing app's bundle id, display name, and on
iOS the device model) and whether it was **entered by hand**. Both are needed
to aggregate honestly, and both are health data under the same rule as the
values.

Android adds one more grant, `READ_HEALTH_DATA_HISTORY`, asked separately.
Without it Health Connect caps every read at thirty days.

## What is written

One `WORKOUT` (HealthKit) / exercise session (Health Connect) per finished
workout, fired after the finish and never awaited or retried:

- start and end, from the session this device holds;
- the activity type, resolved per platform from `WorkoutActivity`
  (`activityOf(workout)`), so a swim is a swim in the user's health record;
- the workout name as the session title, which Health Connect shows and
  HealthKit ignores.

Energy and distance are left nil on purpose. A number Heart estimated, sitting
beside the watch's own measurement and disagreeing with it, is worse than none.

Permissions: `WRITE_EXERCISE` on Android; on iOS the workout type is the only
`WRITE` in the authorisation request. Both usage strings in
`ios/Runner/Info.plist` describe exactly this and nothing wider.

## Where it lives on the device

- **`health_samples`** in `heart.db` (`heart_db` migration 0009), keyed on the
  platform's own sample UUID and scoped by `user_id`. No `synced` column.
- **Backfill markers** in the `syncs` table, `health_backfill:<user>:<metric>`.
  Progress, not data, but wiped alongside it.
- **Two flags** in shared preferences per user: whether the invitation was
  dismissed, and whether the permission sheet has been shown.
- **In memory**, the per-metric daily series the dashboard draws from.

Backups: on Android `heart.db` is excluded from cloud backup and device
transfer (`res/xml/backup_rules.xml`, `data_extraction_rules.xml`). On iOS the
database sits in the app's Documents directory with no backup exclusion, so it
travels with the user's device backup like the rest of the app's data. See
*Open items*.

## User controls and what each one does

| Control                                     | Effect                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
|---------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Dashboard invitation, "Show my health data" | Opens the OS sheet for the six reads plus the workout write; on Android also asks for history access. Dismissable, once.                                                                                      |
| Settings › Health, first tap                | Same sheet. Later taps open the Health app (iOS) or Health Connect settings (Android), because that is where permissions live.                                                                                |
| Settings › "Save workouts to Health"        | Not granted: asks (a sheet appears only if the platform still considers it undetermined). Granted: opens the OS permissions, since turning it off is the platform's to offer. Off means Heart writes nothing. |
| Settings › "Delete health data"             | `Health.forget()`: deletes every mirrored sample and backfill marker for this user. The OS store is untouched; the next sync rebuilds the mirror. Offered only when there is something to delete.             |
| Revoking in the OS                          | Reads return empty. Heart does **not** delete the mirror on that signal, because iOS cannot distinguish "revoked" from "no new readings". The user deletes explicitly.                                        |
| Sign out                                    | Clears the in-memory series and status. Rows stay in `heart.db`, keyed by the signed-out user id, and are invisible to any other account.                                                                     |
| Delete account                              | Removes the server account and workouts. Local health rows are not health data the server ever held, so nothing changes there; they remain until the app is deleted or the user deletes them.                 |
| Delete the app                              | Removes the mirror. The OS store keeps everything it had.                                                                                                                                                     |

## Platform paperwork this policy rests on

**Apple**
- `com.apple.developer.healthkit` in both `.entitlements`; the profile must be
  regenerated when it changes (see the release-gauntlet memory / issue #71).
- `NSHealthShareUsageDescription` and `NSHealthUpdateUsageDescription` in
  `Info.plist`. Both are mandatory (altool 90683 checks the entitlement, not
  the code) and both are shown to users, so they are policy text. Localisation
  is issue #73.
- Guideline 5.1.3: no advertising or data-mining use, no third-party disclosure
  without consent, no health data in iCloud, a privacy policy that says so.

**Google**
- Manifest: the six `READ_*` permissions, `WRITE_EXERCISE`,
  `READ_HEALTH_DATA_HISTORY`, the `ACTION_SHOW_PERMISSIONS_RATIONALE` filter and
  the `VIEW_PERMISSION_USAGE` alias for Android 14's "why does this app have my
  health data" route.
- The Play Console Health Connect declaration must list every permission above
  and match the privacy policy. Reading and writing are declared separately.
  Any new permission means a new declaration review, measured in weeks.

## Change protocol

Touching any of the following is a policy change, and the checklist is the
same each time:

- a metric enters or leaves `Health.tracked`;
- anything new is written to the store;
- a health value or derivative is stored anywhere but `heart.db`, or sent
  anywhere at all (a server column, an analytics event, a notification
  payload);
- the deletion or sign-out behaviour above changes.

Then, in order: update this file; update the Android manifest and the two iOS
usage strings; update `site/privacy.html` in heart-api and its *Last updated*
date; refresh the Play Console declaration; and, for iOS, check whether the
entitlement changed and the provisioning profile needs regenerating.

## Open items

- **iOS backup exclusion.** `heart.db` is excluded from backups on Android and
  not on iOS. Marking it `NSURLIsExcludedFromBackupKey` (or moving it under
  `Library/Caches`) would make the two platforms agree and take the local
  mirror out of iCloud Backup, which is the reading of 5.1.3 that costs
  nothing. The privacy policy currently states the iOS behaviour as it is.
- **Stale comment.** `HealthSampleStore.deleteHealthSamples` says it "runs on
  sign-out". It does not; only the settings delete calls it. Either the
  comment or the behaviour should change, and the table above documents the
  behaviour.
- **Orphaned rows after account deletion.** A deleted account's health rows
  stay in `heart.db` under the old user id until the app is removed. Harmless
  under this policy, but `forget()` on the account-deletion path would be
  tidier.
- **Energy columns.** Decide whether `Workout.calories` and
  `WorkoutExercise.met` will ever be populated. If not, removing them from
  `heart_models` and the schema closes the question for good.
