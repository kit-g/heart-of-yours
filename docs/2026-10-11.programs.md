# Programs: the design

A program is templates on a schedule, for the weeks ahead, with targets that grow as you do.
The sheet stays the one you know. This is the first feature after the 1.13 baseline, the one
that is for everyone, and the bridge to the opt-in world: solo today, alongside someone else
when you choose, from a coach later. The boards are on the Heart Programs design canvas (a
private claude.ai design artifact); this document holds the decisions. The request on the
backend side is heart-api#86. Movement data under it: `docs/2026-10-11.pattern-lens.md`.

## Who it is for

Everyone, not lifters. Someone starting out, someone getting back after an injury with the
plan a physio gave them, someone eating less and trying to keep what they have, and someone
chasing a heavier bar. Heart helps make looking after yourself a bit more affordable. The
free library is grouped by who a program is for, never by lifting style, and Heart never
prescribes rehab: it holds the plan you were given and keeps the counts.

Opt-in, under `docs/opt-in.md`. The ask sits in context: one "Programs" row under the example
templates on the Workout tab, and tapping it asks once. A together link arriving is the other
ask moment. Off, the Workout tab is the 1.13 tab untouched.

## The entity

1. **A program is a parent to templates, not a folder with a schedule.** It carries what a
   folder never has: the schedule, the training maxes, the growth rule per exercise, an
   optional constraint, and where it came from. A folder stays filing. "Make a program from
   this folder" is the bridge and costs one screen.
2. **Where you are is computed, never stored.** A workout started from a program, and a skip
   ("Not today", #312, synced as heart-api#202), each name the program session they belong
   to. The current week and the next session are worked out from that log. Nothing drifts:
   delete a workout and the position follows, and two devices can never disagree.
3. **Its sessions are templates, ours or yours.** A free program runs on Heart's templates as
   they are, the way example templates already do. "Copy and change" makes them yours, in a
   folder named after the program. The first change to one of ours makes the same copy, so a
   Heart template never changes under someone else's feet and the user's veto is always just
   editing.
4. **Schedule is rotation or weekdays.** In rotation the next session is always next, after a
   skip or a missed day alike. On weekdays each session has its day, and the program asks once,
   not per skip, whether a skipped session is dropped or moved. A skip never counts against
   progression or a training max.
5. **Length** is a number of weeks or ongoing. The last session of a fixed program is the goal
   moment below.

## Where it lives

A band at the top of the Workout tab, above Templates: the name, week N of M, this week's
sessions as a strip with done, skipped and next, "Up next" with the first target spelled out
and a one-line rationale, Start, and "Not today" beside it. The program page behind the band
holds the weeks grid, the maxes, the rule in words, and "Follow it with someone".

## Targets on the sheet

The sheet keeps its bones: the same columns, the same 44pt rows, the columns the exercise's
category already produces. A program adds targets as prefilled values in the faint ink, and
one accent line under the exercise name that says the target and where it comes from ("3×8 at
46 kg, 80% of your max"; "hold 30 s, up from 20 s in week 1"). AMRAP reads "8+".

Three states must not look like two. A target is never saved. A tick on a ghost row accepts the
target as the set. A typed value without a tick is not done. Finish names the unticked sets.
Hevy counts copied values as done and never mentions unticked sets; that is the failure to
design against.

Previous, inside a program, is the last time this session, not the last time anywhere. That
makes #172 a near neighbour of programs rather than an idea.

## How it grows

A growth rule belongs to an exercise, not to the program. The program's choice is the default
and lands only where it fits: weight rules on exercises with a weight column, time rules on
holds and walks, the rest stays as written. Each exercise can take its own rule, so one week
holds a heavier bench and a longer walk. The rules:

| Rule | Reads | Grows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Not at all | as written, every time | nothing; Previous still shows |
| Add weight | a step after every completed session | 2.5 kg upper, 5 kg lower by default |
| Rep range, then weight | a range such as 6–10 | weight, once every set reaches the top |
| Share of a max | sets as a share of a training max, a 5+ last set | the max, when the 5+ set is made; down 10% after two misses |
| Longer each week | holds, walks, rounds | a step the user sets: 5 s, 5 min, one round |

Training maxes exist only for "share of a max", suggested at about 90% of the estimated
one-rep max from the user's best sets, and always editable. When a session moves a max, the
Done page says so and offers "Keep" as the only action. It moves unless you say otherwise.

## Done

The existing Done page, unchanged: the motto, hearts for the week, Congratulations, goals
reached, records, the workout card, Ok!, muscles. The program adds one block beside the
records, in the same centred text: the week and session count, what moved and the Keep link,
and what is next. No new card shape.

## Goals

Goals say where, programs say how. A program never stores a target; a goal never stores a
schedule; the forecast is computed from both. They meet in four places:

- **Starting.** A program implies goals and shows them, labelled "from Upper / Lower" on the
  goals card, derived while it runs and never written as goal rows behind the user's back.
  Four sessions a week is a `workouts` goal at the program's cadence; a share-of-a-max rule
  over 12 weeks is an `estimatedOneRepMax` goal dated at the end; longer each week on a walk is
  a `cardioDuration` goal. The reverse is the beginner's entrance: a target and a date, and
  the library says which programs get there in time.
- **During.** Progress needs nothing new; the app computes it from workouts and the Done page
  shows achievements. The program adds the forecast: a goal knows the distance, the program
  knows the slope and the adherence, so the program page and the goal card can both say "at
  85, on track for week 11". Skips move the forecast.
- **Ending.** The last session is a goal moment. Reached goals offer "Again, heavier", which
  restarts from the new maxes. Derived goals not reached offer "Keep as a goal", the same
  shape as "Keep 57.5 kg".
- **Through the pattern lens.** A goal keyed to a pattern can check a program before it
  starts: this one never pulls.

Health goals stay device-only (`docs/2026-09-05.health-data.md`). A program may show a weight
goal's progress beside its own, on the phone, and nothing about it leaves the phone.

## Together

Two columns and nothing else: you and the other person, week, sessions, and whatever the
program grows, each with its change since the start. Maxes for a share-of-a-max program,
holds or sessions for the others, pattern bests when the two programs differ. No likes, no
feed, no ranks. Both people see the same table from their own side; nobody else does. "Stop
following together" keeps your program. The invite flow is not drawn yet. Opt-in, separately
from programs themselves. Copy is gender-neutral throughout; the fixture buddy is Muffin.

## The library

Free, generically named so no named program's licensing is in play, grouped by who it is for:
Starting out, Getting back, Eating less and keeping muscle, Getting stronger. A program page
shows its sessions with their sets and how it grows before "Start it" or "Copy and change".
Preview before commit: Hevy's Trainer asks nine questions and shows a summary; the library asks
nothing and shows the plan. One filter row for equipment, gym, home, nothing, is worth more to
a beginner than a questionnaire. Counts show work sets only; warm-ups are the calculator's
business (#158).

## The user's veto

Editing a template is most of it, and copy-on-write makes it safe on Heart's templates too.
"Replace" on the sheet's exercise menu offers `alternativesTo`: same movement pattern first,
nearest in load after, which is better than Hevy's same muscle. A program may carry a
constraint, "avoid high axial load", that filters every suggestion inside it and leaks nowhere
else. That is the constraint-aware substitution dropped in August, in the scope where it is
sound.

## Coaches

Implied by the model, not built. Templates already carry `assignedBy` and `isAssigned`, so a
coach's program is the same entity arriving over a connection. The board on the canvas is
documentation. A coach sees what the student logs and never their health data.

## Prerequisites and order

1. **#173, targets and ranges on a template set**, is the prerequisite and the first slice. A
   template set today holds one value per column plus set type and RPE, so a template cannot
   say "6 to 10" or "30 s". It is a fine 1.13-baseline feature on its own.
2. **#172**, Previous from the same template, is the near neighbour.
3. **#159**, supersets, is not a prerequisite. The program model leaves room for a group on
   template exercises; circuits matter most to the starting-out and getting-back programs and
   come after.
4. Not filed yet, and programs will want them: replace with suggestions on the sheet, a
   program-scoped constraint, a progress chart per template (the progress view for an
   as-written program where no max moves).
5. heart-api#86 is the backend side: the program entity, sessions that point at templates,
   the position computed from workouts and skips, "share of a max" as a target, and the
   program read-only in the developer API and MCP. Its order: heart-api#195, #197, #196,
   then a backend design doc for #86 built together, then #202 (skips) and #198 (the
   library).

## Not designed yet

Tablet and two-pane, calendar integration, the invite flow for Together, Siri and the "Not
today" shortcut, and what the band looks like on the last week of a fixed program.

## Analytics

Events, under `docs/2026-09-24.analytics.md`: program started (source: library, folder, copy),
session started from a program, session skipped, program finished, together started and
stopped. Identifiers only: a library program's id, never its name; never a max, never a
health value.
