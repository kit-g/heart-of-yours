# Coaching: what the app needs from the API

A request from the app to heart-api, for the first version of coaching. It says what has to exist,
and how it's built is heart-api's call. Where a requirement is firm, the reason is beside it. The
product decisions behind this are in `docs/2026-10-31.coaching.md` in heart-of-yours. The first
version is a free beta, then a release: moderation is in and payment is out.

heart-api answered this in its accepted design, `docs/2026-11-01.coaching.md`, and this request has been
brought into line with it. What was built, including the web invitation surface from section 1, is in
`docs/2026-11-07.coaching-api.md` there, and was handed over in heart-of-yours#552 to #560. Neither is
final. Further needs go to heart-api as new requests, and this document stays the record of the
first one.

Words used here: a **coach** and a **student** are the two sides of an active connection. A
**session** is a template that belongs to a program. A **program copy** is the one a particular
student holds.

## 1. Invitations

A coach invites one named person with a link. There's no way to find a person by name, email or
username, because a coach account must not work as a directory of users.

- A link works for one person and expires after 7 days. Opening it uses nothing up, and only
  accepting does, because someone may tap it, install the app and tap it again.
- A coach can list their pending invitations, and cancel or resend one.
- An invitation can name a program to start with, delivered on acceptance.
- A signed-out web page and the app can both read what the ask needs: the coach's name and photo,
  and whether the link is still good. Nothing else about the coach or anyone else.
- Inviting and accepting work equally from the web and the app. heart-of.me has to answer the link
  with a page that can do both, including signing in, for someone with or without the app or an account.
- Someone who accepts on the web may have no device holding keys yet. The coach can see the connection
  and send a program, and has to be able to tell that the other person has no device yet.
- An invitation names a domain. Accepting one rewrites that domain's row if there is one, with a new
  acceptance date and nothing shared.
- Accepting records what the student also shares, earlier workouts and goals. The default is neither,
  and the student can change it at any time.
- A student at their program limit still connects, and the answer says the program didn't arrive.
- Declining is possible, and the coach sees that it was declined. Someone with only a link can block
  the inviter.
- A limit on invitations per coach, in a form that can tell a busy coach from an abusive one.

Later, not in this version: a public link a coach posts on social media, where a stranger can only
ask and the coach approves each person. A first-version invitation shouldn't assume that it always
names one person and works once.

## 2. What a coach can read

Today an active connection opens everything, to coach and peer alike. A coach needs a narrower view,
and peers stay as they are.

A coach can read: workouts from acceptance on (earlier ones only if the student chose that), with
their notes and sets; the program copy and its position; goals over workout metrics, if the student
chose that; and the name and photo the student uses in Heart. Anything derived, such as position and
bases, is worked out only from what the coach can read. A coach's view beats a peer's.

A coach can't read: any workout the student has hidden, photos, an email address, or a skip's
reason. A skip reason can say `sick` or `sore`, so it's health-shaped, and this is a filter on what a
coach reads. Go-easy areas stay refused by the server, as now.

A coach can read that a session was skipped, repeated or paused, and when.

While a connection is paused, the coach gets no workouts logged during the pause, and sees that it's
paused. It stays paused until the student resumes it. A student's "off" for coaching is a pause.

## 3. Hiding a workout

The student can hide any one of their workouts, from coaches and peers alike, and undo that. A hidden workout is
absent from everything the coach can reach, including by its id, the roster and the feed.

## 4. The roster

A paged list of a coach's clients, for rosters from one person to about a thousand. Per client:

- name and photo;
- program name and position, or none;
- their recent training times, from which the app works out the last 7 days in the coach's zone;
- when they last trained;
- why they need a word, if they do: a session lighter than planned, a new workout note, a gap in
  training, or a program ending next week. More than one can apply, and the app words them.

It can be sorted by need, with those reasons first, and filtered by reason. A coach can mark a client as
reviewed, and reasons count only what came after that. A student never sees any of this about themselves.
Someone coached in two domains appears twice.

## 5. The feed and coach pushes

A paged feed across a coach's clients, newest first, of finished workouts, skips and programs
ending. It respects sections 2 and 3.

Pushes never contain workout content, message text or health values, and can use names ("Alex
messaged you"), because they pass through Apple and Google. Workout news reaches a coach at most once
a day, in one push, and a message pushes at once. No push ever nudges a student to train.

The app draws its push settings from the server's list of types, localized, with each one's delivery:
instant, daily or off, per type and per person. A new type needs no app release. Devices can send a UTC
offset, and silent pushes carry changes the app has to look at.

## 6. A coach's programs

- A coach has a library of programs kept apart from their personal templates and programs, so
  coaching work doesn't appear among their own training.
- A coach can send a library program to one or several clients. Each gets their own copy with its own
  ids, and without bases, which the student's app proposes from their history.
- Changing a library program changes no copy. The library program knows who holds which version, and
  a coach can bring chosen copies up to date in one action. That keeps the ids of what the student has
  trained, and overwrites their own edits as a change that can be put back.
- Changing one student's copy leaves the library program alone.
- A program that would replace what a student is on waits for them to say when it starts. Otherwise
  it can start at once.
- When a program ends, the student keeps it.

## 7. Two people editing one program copy

While the connection is active, the coach and the student can both change the student's copy.

- Every change records who made it, when and what it was, and both can read that history. It has to
  survive the session being edited or deleted afterwards.
- A coach can put a student's change back, and that's a change like any other. Putting back a deleted
  session relinks its workouts and skips.
- Neither side may silently overwrite the other. Conflicts are per session and only with the other
  person, and answer `409 program_changed`. A retried save repeats its id. A build too old to send a base
  revision has its write accepted and marked stale, and nothing is lost.
- The student's app learns that the program changed without a full refetch, and can tell what changed.
- A coach's write access to a copy ends when the connection it was sent through stops being active.

## 8. Target beside done

A coach's review compares what was asked with what was done. The app records the planned session on the
workout when it's saved, because lighter cycles and wave rules mean the stored session can't say what was
asked, and it has to survive later edits. Each set can be tied to the planned set it came from. Added sets
have no tie, and planned sets with none are what wasn't done. A workout with no plan, from an older build
or not from a program, has no comparison.

A finished workout never changes a program's sessions.

## 9. Logging for someone else

A coach can log a workout for a client, in person. It's the student's workout: it appears in their
history, counts toward their program, is marked as logged by the coach, and is never public. The
student can delete it. Only an active connection allows it. A coach's custom exercise comes along, and the
coach's device posts the base move, as any device does.

## 10. Leaving and blocking

Either side can end a connection at once, in one step.

- The student keeps their program copy as their own, with no further coach edits, plus their history
  and the thread.
- The coach keeps that the person was a client, the dates, the thread and their notes. They lose
  everything else, including what they could read before. A former client appears on a list of their
  own. Only a new invitation reopens a coach relationship.
- A block belongs to the pair, across every domain and every cross-user path. A blocked person can't
  read, message or invite, and can't undo the block. The history of a block survives it being lifted.
- Only the student resumes a paused coach connection.

## 11. Reports

Apple's guideline 1.2 and Google's equivalent require a way to report and block, and a way to act on
reports, for anything users send each other.

- A student or a coach can report the other, with a reason and the words their device sends. The words
  can hold anything, so they're never stored in clear: the server checks them, then keeps them sealed to
  a key whose private half isn't on the server. Someone at Heart opens them with a script and acts on
  them, and `site/support.html` is the contact route. Reports outlive either person's account, and go
  90 days after closing or after a year.
- The server must be able to tell whether reported messages are genuine. Section 12 has what that
  takes.

## 12. Messages and notes

**Messages.** One thread per connection, text only.

- The server keeps every message, once, for as long as the thread exists: sending, a paged read in
  either direction, and fetching what's new since a point. Every device of both people reads the same
  stored copy, and can read it again.
- Reads go by the server's order, and the app shows the device's time, so a message written offline
  can't fall behind a reader.
- Message content is opaque to the server, which never reads, indexes, logs or filters it. Encryption is
  from the first message, with no version in the clear. A message can be limited to 16 KB: a 500-character
  one was about 800 characters encoded in the spike.
- A message can name what it's about beside the content: a workout, exercise, set, photo, skip, program or
  session. It's a link that opens nothing, and it can be searched by.
- A message has a kind, and the first version allows only `message`.
- Either person can delete the whole conversation for both, whatever the connection's status. Every device
  learns from the thread, and the app keeps its copy for 7 days for reporting only.
- Unread counts per thread and overall, for the inbox, from a read position per person.
- After a connection ends, the thread takes no new messages and stays readable by both. Deleting an
  account leaves it with the other person.
- The `/me` API, MCP and analytics don't serve or see messages or notes. The assistant can't read
  them.
- Existing comments stay as they are, with no client, and the thread is the inbox. Comment pushes lose
  their text.

**Notes.** A coach keeps notes per client, readable by that coach only, on all their own devices. The
client can't see that they exist. The content is opaque as above. Notes stay when a client leaves or deletes their account, until the coach
deletes them or the coach's account goes. A note about someone's knee is health-shaped, so this is the
first health-shaped remote copy of anything, even if unreadable, and `site/privacy.html` must say so
before any tester reaches it. The doc in heart-of-yours changes with it.

**What encryption needs,** from the spike:

- **Keys per device.** A device publishes two public keys of about 43 characters each, up to 50
  single-use keys of the same size, and one fallback key, and replaces them as they're used. Another
  person's device can fetch a person's device list and take one single-use key per device, if the two
  share a thread and there's no block, including after a connection has ended. No
  single-use key is ever handed out twice, the fallback is used once they run out, and a device can
  learn how many it has left. A device can be removed.
- **Vouching.** A new device can carry a signature from one its owner already has. The other person's
  app is told about any new device, and whether it was vouched for.
- **Key delivery.** The keys that open a thread reach each device one at a time, sealed for that
  device. A device fetches what's waiting for it, oldest first, in the order sent, and confirms it.
  Order is firm for these: in the spike, reading 50 such items newest first lost 9 for good. A
  confirmed item can be dropped, and an unconfirmed one is kept for at least 90 days. An item is under
  1 KB. A sending device sends one at a time, and a pair can have at most 2,000 unconfirmed items.
- **Asking again and starting over.** A device missing a key can ask every other device in the thread
  for it, and one that can't open an item can ask its sender to start again. Both go by the same
  route, as items the server can't read, and neither needs anything more from the server.
- **Genuine reports.** With each message the sender gives the server a 32-byte commitment. The server
  makes a tag from that commitment, the sender, the recipient and the time, using a key only it holds,
  and delivers the tag and time with the message. Given the words, a key from the reporter, the
  commitment and the tag, it can confirm that this sender sent those words, without having been able
  to read them. Tested in the spike, including altered words and a blamed third person.

## 13. Opt-in

Under `docs/opt-in.md` in heart-of-yours, "Coach people" and "Your coach" are two features whose state
syncs like the others. A feature being off changes no server behaviour by itself and deletes nothing,
except that the app pauses the student's connection, as in section 2. Accepting, on the web or in the app, leaves "Your coach"
on for that account, so that the app agrees when it next opens. In the beta, only an account holding the `coach` entitlement, granted by hand, can
create an invitation, and the profile says whether it holds it. Anyone with a link can accept.

## Firm constraints

- **No health data in anything here.** The reason is the device-only rule in
  `docs/2026-09-05.health-data.md`.
- **Old builds must survive this.** Builds before coaching ignore what they don't understand and must
  not lose it on a save. An old build opened by a student on a program copy must not erase the coach's
  work. The app's compatibility floor is 1.13.
- **`site/privacy.html` changes before any tester reaches this.** It says today that we don't share, and it has to
  describe coaching, and what a coach can and can't see.
- **Pending and declined connections open nothing.** This is as it is today
  (`docs/2026-08-02.connections.md`), and has to remain true of everything above.
